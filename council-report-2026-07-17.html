Council Report — Maridive Fleet Vessels App

Professional-mariner review of the whole app: what to add, what to amend, before fleet-wide rollout · 17 July 2026

The question: The app manages 5 OSVs (Maridive 601, 701, 704, Zohr 1, Zohr 2) from Tripoli with 16 modules — tanks, defects, requisitions, port calls, certifications, notifications, tasks, crew, handovers, logbook, exports, file viewer, AI extraction, analytics. As professional mariners: what additions or amendments are required for it to serve as the fleet's primary daily operations tool, measured against real OSV workflows and ISM/SOLAS/MLC/class/flag expectations?

Chairman's Verdict

Where the council agrees

All five advisors independently converged on the same three gaps — the strongest possible signal:

  1. Planned Maintenance System (PMS) is the single biggest missing module. Running-hours-driven maintenance for critical machinery (ISM 10.3) is what a chief engineer lives in. Without it the app stays the "second screen" next to Excel.
  2. A daily noon report is the missing spine. One 3-minute form — soundings, ROB, running hours, position, weather, activity mode — feeds tanks, PMS counters, dashboard, and analytics at once, and cuts the data-entry burden that decides adoption.
  3. Sync trust and infrastructure honesty. Per-record sync indicators, a defined conflict-resolution policy for concurrent offline edits, and either a paid database tier (~$25/mo) or an explicit "paper remains the system of record" stance.

Secondary consensus: AI extraction needs a logged human-verification step (one hallucinated certificate date can detain a vessel), and rollout should be a one-vessel pilot with a hard adoption gate.

Where the council clashes

Rebuild vs. ship: First Principles wants the data model unified around deviations/readiness/daily-report before anything else; the Executor calls that an unshippable rewrite for a solo developer. Resolution: add the unified "Outstanding" view as a new screen over existing data — no rewrite.

Consolidate vs. expand: the Outsider says fewer modules done boringly well; the Expansionist says double down on tank data as bunker-dispute intelligence and design for Maridive's ~80-vessel fleet. Peer review sided 4-to-1 with consolidation first — but the bunker-reconciliation insight survives as the best reason the tank module deserves investment.

Blind spots the council caught

  1. Organizational mandate is the real gate: under ISM, an app is only "primary" if the company's SMS designates it (controlled forms, DPA sign-off, flag acceptance of e-records). Otherwise it's an uncontrolled parallel record — itself an audit finding.
  2. Record integrity: editable historical entries are worse than paper in an audit or casualty investigation. Logbook-class records need append-only history with visible corrections and master sign-off.
  3. Security & access control: crew PII and certificates in cloud storage, row-level security, per-user accounts and crew offboarding — unaddressed in round one.
  4. Premature scaling: 4 of 5 reviewers rejected multi-tenant/market ambitions before single-vessel adoption is proven.

The recommendation

Do not add more feature modules yet. Make four amendments that convert the 16 modules from a records portfolio into an operations tool:

  1. Ship a daily noon-report form feeding tanks, running hours, and the dashboard.
  2. Build the PMS module on those running hours (top ~30 machinery items per vessel).
  3. Add a unified per-vessel "Outstanding" view — open defects + expiring certs + active alarms + assigned actions in one accountable, sign-off list.
  4. Harden trust: sync-status indicators, append-only record history, AI-verification step, paid database tier.

In parallel, get Maridive management to formally sanction the app in the SMS — that decision, not any feature, determines whether it becomes the primary tool.

The one thing to do first

Ship the noon-report form and pilot it on one vessel (the friendliest master), with a single success metric: 30 consecutive days of daily submissions before building anything else.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — "A records system pretending to be an operations tool"

Connectivity is your fatal flaw. Offshore Tripoli means VSAT that drops for days, and "offline-first with Supabase free tier" means five vessels syncing photo evidence, PDFs, and readings through a plan with row limits, 1GB storage, and projects that pause after a week of inactivity. Your sync will silently fail mid-hitch, two masters will edit the same defect list, and your conflict resolution strategy is — what, exactly?

No planned maintenance system. For a chief engineer, PMS is the job — running hours, class-linked intervals, critical equipment per ISM 10.3, spares tied to jobs. Without it the chief double-enters, and double entry is how ops apps die.

Regulatory records without controls are worse than paper. Can a master retroactively edit a logbook entry? If yes, no auditor accepts it and it's a liability in a casualty investigation. Where's the sign-off workflow, the DPA reporting line, the non-conformity/corrective-action loop? A defect list is not a nonconformity system.

Missing the daily bread: noon reports, ROB/consumption reconciliation, drill and rest-hour logging (PSC's favorite deficiency), crew change planning against Libyan visa chaos, DP/anchor-handling job records.

And the AI extraction? One hallucinated certificate expiry a superintendent trusts, and a vessel gets detained. Who verifies, and where is that logged? Ask the crews what they'd stop using Excel for. I'd bet the answer today is: nothing.

First Principles — "Is this vessel safe, legal, and ready to work today?"

Fleet software on an OSV operation exists to answer one question reliably: "Is this vessel safe, legal, and ready to work today — and if not, who must do what by when?" Everything else is decoration.

This module list scatters. A defect, an overdue certificate, a tank alarm, an urgent notification, and a daily task are all the same underlying object — a deviation requiring an owner, a deadline, and a closure record. The app models them as five silos: no unified "what's outstanding on Maridive 704" view, no accountability chain, no audit trail an ISM auditor can walk.

The most glaring absence is what ISM actually runs on: planned maintenance. Tank readings without engine running hours and consumption-vs-ROB reconciliation is half a picture. If the PMS lives elsewhere, this app will never be "primary" — it will be the second screen.

Second absence: this is a charter business. No charter/DPR module, no off-hire tracking, no client-facing deliverable — "are my five vessels earning?" is unanswerable here.

Rebuild around three primitives: deviations (unified lifecycle with sign-off), readiness (certs + PMS + crew compliance as one per-vessel status), and the daily operational report. The current modules become views on those, not islands.

The Expansionist — "The tank module is the undervalued crown jewel"

Tank soundings plus readings history is 80% of the way to bunkering intelligence — the single biggest cost line on an OSV. Add ROB trend curves, consumption-per-mode (DP, transit, standby, port), and charterer fuel reconciliation, and this settles bunker disputes worth tens of thousands of dollars per voyage. Charterers (Eni, Mellitah) demand daily fuel reporting anyway — auto-generate their format and the app becomes contractually embedded.

The missing multiplier: PMS. Ship it and the app touches every crew member every day — that's the retention lock-in.

Next-order upside: (1) noon reports with auto-computed distance, consumption, DP hours; (2) crew rotation planning tied to the existing certification module (MLC rest hours + rotation forecasting); (3) free AIS + Open-Meteo overlays on the fleet dashboard.

The real prize: Maridive operates ~80 vessels. Bilingual Arabic-first, offline-tolerant OSV software has essentially zero competitors in the Egyptian/Libyan/Gulf market. Build the noon report + PMS + bunker triangle, prove it on five hulls, and you have the fleet-wide standard. Design the schema multi-tenant now.

The Outsider — "Sixteen modules is a portfolio, not a product"

Ask what a master actually opens daily — probably tanks, defects, and tasks — and make everything else secondary. Offshore crews rotate constantly, so onboarding is the normal condition: you need role-based home screens (master / chief / superintendent) and a 10-minute path to competence, or each rotation resets adoption to zero.

1. Bus factor of one. Before adding modules, write down how sync conflicts resolve, how to restore a vessel's data, and how to onboard a second maintainer.

2. Free tier is not an operations tier. Supabase free pauses inactive projects, caps storage, no SLA. Either budget ~$25/month or explicitly define the app as a convenience layer with paper as the system of record.

3. Data-entry burden decides everything. Every field you add is a field someone fakes at 2 a.m. Audit which entries are used downstream and delete the rest.

4. Trust needs visibility. Users must always see per-record sync status — "did shore actually receive this?" — or they fall back to WhatsApp, which is your real competitor.

Fewer modules, done boringly well, beats sixteen done fragile.

The Executor — "Ship the thing they'll open every morning"

Week 1: Noon report as a single 3-minute form — tank soundings, ROB, running hours, position, weather. That one screen feeds dashboard, tanks, and analytics for free. Sync-status indicators everywhere. Seed all 5 vessels' data yourself — never ask crews to bootstrap an empty app.

Month 1: Running-hours-based PMS for the top ~30 machinery items per vessel, wired to noon-report counters. Certificate expiry escalation (30/60/90 days). PDF export matching the exact formats charterers and PSC inspectors already expect.

Quarter 1: Requisition-to-delivery tracking ("where's my spare part"), oil-record-book-style logs, drill/safety meeting scheduler with photo evidence, then Android — masters do rounds with phones, not laptops.

Do NOT build: full ISM document management, e-signatures, crewing/payroll, charter party management, custom reporting engines, or more AI features until the data underneath is trustworthy.

Rollout: one friendly-master pilot vessel, two weeks, personal WhatsApp support, 10-minute Arabic training video, one laminated sheet per module. Adoption metric: all 5 vessels submitting a noon report daily for 30 straight days before building anything new.

Peer-review highlights

What the anonymous cross-review surfaced

Strongest-response votes: First Principles ×3 (First Principles, Expansionist, Outsider reviewers), Contrarian ×1 (Contrarian reviewer: "D's flaws are the ones auditors and PSC will actually find"), Executor ×1 (Executor reviewer: "the only response with a Monday-morning plan and a hard adoption gate").

Blind-spot votes: Expansionist ×4 — "a business plan, not a review"; "optimizes the prize before the prerequisite"; "building bunker-dispute evidence on infrastructure that silently fails mid-hitch is a contradiction E never notices". Outsider ×1 (from the Expansionist reviewer: "pure subtraction; caution without a compounding thesis").

What ALL five missed (each caught only in review):

· Organizational mandate — under ISM the SMS must designate the app (controlled forms, DPA sign-off, flag acceptance of electronic records) or it's an uncontrolled parallel record — itself a nonconformity. Ownership, data custody, and what happens if the developer leaves were never addressed.

· Security & privacy — crew PII and certificates in the cloud, row-level security, per-user accounts, crew offboarding, device loss, Libya-specific data-hosting and sanctions-adjacent constraints.

· The data as the asset — consumption-per-mode, defect MTTR, and PMS histories compound into fleet benchmarking, charter-bid costing, and vetting evidence; plus interoperability pipes (API/export) to charterer and class systems.

· Device & language reality — shared vessel PCs vs. per-user logins, and Arabic data-entry quality (not just an RTL UI).
